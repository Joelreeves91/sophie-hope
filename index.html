Easy is a state, not a pace. Easy running is defined by what your body is doing (aerobic, conversational, low lactate), not by what your watch says. On a windy or warm day easy might be 6:50/km. On a cold, recovered morning it might be 6:15/km. Both are correct if HR stays in 139–160. The pace shown for easy sessions is what your Aerobic Endurance zone typically produces right now, not a target. As the block progresses, that pace drops naturally at the same HR.

Why 5:30 on race day but 5:35–5:39 in training? Rome measures 43.2km on the Strava GPX, so 5:30/km on the watch covers the real 42.195km at about 5:37/km: a 3:58. In training your watch reads within about 0.5% of true distance, so the same effort shows as 5:35–5:39. Never run 5:30 on the watch in training. On race day, check the watch against the km markers by 5km; if they disagree, the markers win.

Why no VO2max intervals? The marathon is about 99% aerobic, and your 5K speed already exceeds what Goal A needs. The gap is threshold and endurance, so the hard sessions rotate through threshold formats, hills and marathon-pace work instead.

What This Block Must Deliver

  • Volume: 45–55km a week from Week 8, five runs in most weeks, no blank weeks
  • MP endurance: 18km at 5:35–5:39 inside a 30km long run (Week 18)
  • Threshold ceiling: 5:30–5:34 to 5:15–5:19/km across four tiers
  • Durability: two strength sessions every week; no fade before 40km in the Week 16 and 18 long runs
  • Gut training: 75–80g carbohydrate an hour in every run over two hours from Week 15

Before Week 7

  • Bloods: ask your GP for a ferritin and full blood count check. Low iron is common in female distance runners and would cap every gain this plan is chasing.
  • Cycle: log it in Garmin Connect so key sessions can be moved off your worst days rather than run badly on them.

Use a glucose-plus-fructose gel (Maurten, SiS Beta Fuel, Precision). Dual sugars use two gut transporters, so you absorb more with less distress. Build gradually, and settle the exact race gel by Week 14 so every long run after that is a rehearsal. Never trial anything new in Rome.

Race Pacing: 3-Phase Progressive

Phase 1 (km 0–5): 5:45–5:50/km on the road (5:37–5:42 on a watch reading 43.2km). Let the adrenaline settle and find rhythm.

Phase 2 (km 5–30): 5:36–5:38/km (5:28–5:30 on the watch). Lock the rhythm in and run the tangents.

Phase 3 (km 30–42.2): hold 5:35–5:38/km over the cobbles between 30 and 40km. Shorter stride, same cadence. Push only after 40km.

Daily Flags

  • RHR 5+ bpm above baseline, 2 consecutive mornings: drop to easy. No quality.
  • RHR 8+ bpm above baseline: full rest. Reassess next morning.
  • Pain that changes your stride: stop immediately. Two days off if still present, then tell your coach.
  • Under 5 hours sleep: downgrade quality to easy.
  • Day 1–2 of your period with heavy symptoms: swap the key session with the next easy day. Never stack.
  • Low motivation plus aches for 3+ days: unplanned deload.

Session Signals

  • Easy pace dropping 15+ sec/km at the same HR over a week: fatigue accumulating. Deload or drop the next quality session.
  • Cannot hit T-pace: slow 8 sec/km and call it sub-threshold. Two misses in a row means the tier was set too fast.
  • MP block pushes HR past 175 before its last 3km, twice in a row: move to the next MP band down.
  • Week 18 long run falls apart before 12km of MP: race to the sub-4 band (5:39–5:43), with Goal B as the floor.
  • Three poor quality sessions in a row: mandatory deload, do not push through.

Missed Sessions

Do not stack missed sessions. The next session is whatever was already planned, not the missed one bolted on.

Exception: the Week 16 and Week 18 MP long runs. If one is missed, move it by up to two days and drop that week's other quality. Never run both in the same week.

Race Week Illness

Mild cold, head only, no fever: race goes ahead.

Chest, lungs, or fever: do not race. Marathon stress on a body fighting infection risks myocarditis. There will be other marathons.

Stomach bug 48+ hours before: if fluids have stayed down 24 hours pre-race, start. Otherwise do not.
